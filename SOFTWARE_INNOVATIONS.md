# Software innovations

- **Shoot-on-the-move in layers.** Static aim, then velocity lead (aim at `target - velocity × flight time`, repeated 3 times because the distance changes the flight time), then latency prediction (solve from where the robot will be when the turret catches up). Each layer has its own on/off switch in Panels, so on the field we can always fall back to a simpler one.
- **Infinite-rotation turret with no homing.** An absolute encoder on the turret itself gives the true angle at power-on, and the turret always takes the shortest way around. Separate gain sets for big moves and for settling, and a feedforward that cancels the robot turning underneath it.
- **Shot detection from the flywheel.** Each ball is fed only after the shot has stayed ready for 40 ms. A dip in flywheel RPM confirms the ball hit the wheel, and no dip means a misfeed. The door stays open for the whole burst and only the rail pauses, so the door never closes on a ball.
- **Fast flywheel recovery.** Feedforward plus P control, scaled for battery voltage, switches to full power mid-burst whenever RPM drops. The flywheel pre-spins whenever a ball is stored, so it's at speed before the trigger is pulled.
- **4-slot storage from 3 sensors.** Break beams at slots 1, 2 and 4, debounced by time. "Full" means the last beam stays blocked, not just crossed. The firing rule only needs "2 or more", so slot 3 needs no sensor.
- **Self-protecting mechanisms.** Watchdogs shut the shooter or turret off when their feedback is missing, frozen or wired backwards. The turret checks four ways. If it keeps turning against its own push (the REVERSED flags disagree, so it runs away), it is off within about 1.5 s (about a third of a second when the target is near), even while the robot turns, because the encoder measures the turret against the chassis. Full power that doesn't close the error turns it off after 1.5 s, as a backup. If it pushes but the angle doesn't move, it pushes harder, little by little: friction gives way and the turret moves on, and a turret that still doesn't move rests for a second and tries again. So a turret held for a few seconds comes back, and a dead encoder is off after three failed tries (about 4 to 6 s). And a push too small to start the turret is cut to 0 once the angle stands still, so an encoder that freezes mid-move can't leave the turret creeping. One case is left: an encoder that freezes right next to the target. The turret stops within half a second, but it believes the frozen reading, since a frozen reading and a turret at rest look the same. A NaN pose blocks aiming (a burst already firing keeps its last good aim, so the ball being fed doesn't go into a slowing wheel), and nothing fires without an alliance and a trusted pose.
- **Safe pose hand-off.** Only trusted poses are saved, and only for 60 s, so auto's pose carries into TeleOp but a guess or a stale pose never does. Switching alliance drops the saved pose.
- **One-button relocalize** in any of the 4 corners on our alliance wall. All poses are written once for red and flipped for blue.
- **One source for driver warnings.** A single list drives both the screen and the rumble. There are only two rumbles (warning, storage full), so each always means the same thing. The gamepad light shows the ball count.
- **Everything tunable live.** Every constant, including the shot tables, direction flags and auto poses, can be edited in Panels while the robot runs.
- **Tuning checklist built from the code.** `// TODO(step): ...` comments are collected into `TODO_LIST.md` on every build, sorted by tuning step.
- **Fast loop.** One bulk read per hub, motor and servo writes only on change, cached battery voltage, no current reads, and a one-switch competition mode that cuts telemetry to warnings every 200 ms. Target: under 15 ms.
- **AgateFlow, our own path planner.** Give it where to go and it finds a way around the HIVE frame, the FLOWERS and anything the camera adds, in the background so the loop never waits (a median of 7 to 15 ms on a desktop). For each candidate path it simulates Pedro's own path follower driving our drivetrain (motor lag, battery sag, grip), keeps the fastest one that stays clear, and adds speed limits only where the robot would cut a corner too close. In simulation its predicted drive times are off by 1 to 2% (median) when the simulated robot matches its model, and 2 to 6% when it doesn't. Random tests of 600 fields each found no planned or predicted path that breaks its clearance rule. If the camera adds something in the way at speed, it sees it the next loop, brakes harder than the follower's own hold (about 7 in. from 52 in/s in simulation), and replans from where it stopped. It has not run on the robot yet.
- **The robot's real shape.** AgateFlow checks the robot's rectangle, not a circle around it, so it may pass things side-on and drive along a wall touching it, but it plans never to move into a wall faster than 10 in/s. Hugging a wall, that made one path 66 in. and 2.2 s instead of 191 in. and 6.9 s. The flower intake's longer reach is planned only where the route lowers it, and the auto command lowers and raises it to match.
- **Picking up in the best order.** For several POLLEN on the floor, it times every order (or finds a near-best one with Held-Karp when there are too many), plans the best few in full, and predicts which ones the intake will really catch. It planned all 300 random simulated scenes and was predicted to catch every target in 299, and its order was within 0.7% of the best one found by trying them all.
- **Auto and TeleOp share one brain.** Auto commands set the same `Robot` inputs the gamepad buttons do, so aiming, firing and safety rules are written once.

Some ideas came from two DECODE (2025-26) teams: [GNCE Ditto](https://github.com/GNCE/DECODEv2-Ditto) and [Meta-Infinity](https://github.com/Meta-Infinity/DecodePublicRelease).
